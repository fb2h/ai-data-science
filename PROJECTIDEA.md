My project will investigate which factors and combinations of factors are associated with a finanically risky college and major choice for students, 
finanically risky meaning when students take on a relatively high amount compared to the earnings they are likely to make after graduating. I will 
examine factors such as tuition, net price, major, graduation rate, finanical aid, school type, selectivity, student debt, Parent PLUS debt, and 
graduate earnings to determine which factors are more strongly connected to a high financial risk. This topic is important because choosing a college 
and major is one of the largest finanical decisions a student and family make and understanding these patterns could help students recognize choices that may lead
to high debt without equal earnings. I expect to use large public datasets from the US Department of Education, mainly college
scorecards and IPEDS. 

Questions and secondary questions:
What college major combinations are financially risky because graduates take on high debt compared with what they earn after graduating?
Have those financial risk patterns changed over time espeically around major economical and technological changes? And can we predict these trends with machine learning?
